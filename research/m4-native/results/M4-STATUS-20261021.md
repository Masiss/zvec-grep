# M4 research status — 2026-10-21

**Overall status: PARTIAL — the M4 engineering goal is not complete.** No architecture has been selected as the final design, and no prototype is ready to be described as satisfying the combined writer-latency, reader-latency, resource, durability, and contention goals. At the owner's request, work stopped after producing this status report; this document records the state and does not authorize further implementation or benchmark execution.

## Current disposition

- Keep **immutable-shard** as the working baseline, not as a proven winner.
- **Defer shared-collection promotion.** It has useful correctness and recovery evidence, but this pair showed high reader latency and material GC cost, with uncontrolled host-I/O pressure and incomplete workspace accounting.
- No cached-fingerprint or other optimization is promoted to the default based on this comparison. Existing independent findings remain in the linked historical reports.

The writer measurements have different boundaries: shared includes GC, whereas the baseline field is commit. There were only five writer cycles per arm, no stationary control or repetition, and the host I/O pressure differed. These are descriptive observations, **not a causal or architecture-wide comparison**. In particular, the lower observed shared writer-cycle p95 does not establish a writer-latency win.

## Source diagnosis and interpretation

The candidate's per-round `gc_m4_search_delete_ms` was 349.8–382.5 ms, within total GC of 373.5–407.3 ms; measured M4 GC compaction was 0 ms in all five rounds. Source inspection shows `Coordinator::Commit` holds the exclusive `snapshot_mutex_` across update, flush, and publish, while `Coordinator::Hybrid` needs the shared lock across both backends. `CollectBeforeGeneration` also holds the exclusive lock and measures the generation `SearchFilter` plus per-hit `Delete` loop in the M4 search/delete stage.

This makes lock wait and per-generation GC discovery plausible targets for investigation. Shared reader wall latency (59.3–97.9 ms) greatly exceeded reader-thread CPU (1.3–3.1 ms), but uncontrolled host pressure and a single run prevent assigning the whole difference to locking. Preserve snapshot, lease, flush-order, and crash-recovery invariants in any future design review.

## Completion gaps

The broader M4 goal called for selecting a design with low writer latency, light resource footprint, robust durability, and practical behavior under contention. This evidence does not close that goal:

1. The immutable-shard/shared comparison is one small exploratory pair with non-equivalent writer timing boundaries; it does not establish a winner or representative tail behavior.
2. Shared reader latency and the GC search/delete path remain unresolved. The pair does not establish their behavior across sustained workloads or other contention levels.
3. The two receipts are `COMPLETED_WITH_WORKSPACE_UNCERTAINTY`; traversals completed without scan errors but were non-atomic. No verified lifetime workspace peak/cap proof is available. Measurement quality is `INCOMPLETE` / non-qualifying; sampled cgroup peaks are observations only.
4. Existing shared-coordinator correctness/crash tests are test-only evidence on bounded fixtures (1,046 coordinator checks and 1,247 full bridge/crash checks as recorded in the research README). They do not by themselves establish production suitability, cross-process lease safety, or full-scale recovery/performance.
5. The combined design decision still needs to weigh writer/reader latency, native and M4 open/update cost, GC and retention debt, memory/tasks, CPU/I/O, and recovery on comparable workloads. No integrated candidate has passed that decision gate.
