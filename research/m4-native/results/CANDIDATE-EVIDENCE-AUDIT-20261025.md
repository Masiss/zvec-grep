# M4 candidate and evidence audit — 2026-10-25

## Scope and conclusion

This audit reconciles the current native implementation and regression tests with the newest immutable-write-amplification pairs and the earlier compaction/group-fsync evidence. Source and working-tree behavior take precedence over older handoffs. No production/default behavior is changed, and no benchmark was launched for this audit.

**Disposition:** retain `ApplyBatch` only for callers that accept one atomic transaction; keep WAL-LZ4 and cap5 research-only; leave stats coalescing off pending a lower-cost implementation; retain mutation chunk 128 and compaction cap4 as controls; reject LZ4 acceleration 4 and compaction cap6; keep per-Upsert group-fsync research-only and do not integrate it into the current watcher. These dispositions are conservative: the performance reports are explicitly non-qualifying, not proof of production wins or regressions.

## Measurement and interpretation audit

- Arm order was counterbalanced for the newest chunk, LZ4, acceleration, and coalescing pairs. This reduces a simple order bias; it does not remove cache/page-cache warming, evolving filesystem state, prior compaction debt, or background host load. The sequential arms are not independent randomized repetitions.
- Host snapshots caught concurrent LSP/indexing/agent/browser activity. Sparse before/after snapshots cannot reconstruct load during each request. All discussed paired reports say `performance_eligible=false`; latency/CPU differences are descriptive, not causal performance results.
- “Process-attributed writes” are process/cgroup accounting (`/proc`/process-tree write bytes), not block-device writes, filesystem metadata amplification, or physical-media writes. WAL bytes, stats-log bytes, segment bytes, process writes, and Btrfs qgroup referenced usage are distinct counters and must not be conflated.
- WAL compression's per-frame bytes and compressor CPU are mechanism-level evidence. They do not include all decompression/open costs or establish end-to-end durability latency. Coalescing's stats-log reduction is real; its total-cost trade is still uncertain under contention.
- The cap5 runs were not debt-drained; cap6's immediate write reduction similarly does not establish lifetime cost. A useful comparison must include post-churn compaction/debt drain, query fan-out, reader tails, peak RSS, and total bytes through the drain.
- Btrfs quota recovery was traced to reflinked archived operation trees retaining qgroup extents. Those trees were rematerialized by buffered non-reflink copies and manifest-verified; cap stayed 128 MiB. Future archive copy semantics are part of resource lifecycle, not benchmark behavior.

## Source-level follow-up discovered during this audit

The WAL replay read loop treated every `pread` result `<= 0` as EOF, then resized the input buffer to the partial byte count. A transient `EINTR` or other read error at a frame boundary could therefore be returned as successful replay of only the prefix, leaving the caller unaware that later durable records were not applied. This is a concrete recovery failure mode, not a benchmark hypothesis. The current working-tree fix retries `EINTR`, returns an error on other read failures, and fails closed on unexpected EOF; focused fault-injection tests cover one interrupted read, a hard read error, and successful recovery after the error. The pre-fix path is preserved only in Git history; no operation data or benchmark was touched.

A separate unmeasured resource hypothesis remains: `Wal::Replay` reads the entire WAL into one `std::string`, then materializes decoded records in a `std::vector<WalRecord>` before applying them; compressed bytes and decompressed payloads coexist. `CollectUncommittedDeletedIds` also calls `Replay` in durable-rebase paths. This may cause avoidable replay peak-RSS and allocation/copy costs. The next bounded engineering slice is to audit these lifetimes and quantify a peak-memory model, then—if justified—consider a streaming frame decoder preserving legacy/LZ4 support, checksums, torn-suffix repair, sequence ordering and crash semantics. Measure replay-open/peak RSS separately from writer ACK; do not start a general A/B or repeat a completed profile just to fill a matrix.
